# Proximity voice, routed by the server and carried browser to browser

Players can talk. In the Lobby, the Role Reveal, every Meeting and the finished Game everyone hears everyone. During a Roam you hear only living Players near you, louder the closer they are and panned toward the side they stand on. Ghosts hear each other anywhere.

**Audio goes browser to browser over WebRTC; the server routes it.** Every voice-enabled Member keeps a peer connection with every other one in the Room. The game socket carries only the negotiation (`voice_signal`) and each Member's `voice_routes`: for every peer, whether this client may send it its voice and whether it may play that peer's. A route is a server decision made by `Game.hears` under the Room lock, re-evaluated on every field tick and every transition, and sent only when it changes. Relaying the audio through a media server instead (an SFU) would enforce routes on the server too, but it would add an external service and a hop of latency to a game whose server keeps everything in memory and plays mostly on one network. Ten Players need at most nine peer connections each: about 0.6 Mbit/s up at 64 kbit/s Opus.

**The speaker enforces the route.** A client sends its microphone track only to peers whose route allows it, and swaps it out (`replaceTrack(null)`) the moment a route closes, so a modified listening client cannot hear what an honest speaker never sends. The listener also refuses to play a peer it may not hear. A modified speaking client could broadcast, but that exposes only its own voice.

**So routes must not reveal anything the views do not already show,** because the speaker learns its routes too. Hearing reaches 260 px, inside every Vision, and needs both Players to see each other. Whoever you can hear, you can already see, and they can see you. A Vanished Mafia is silent to the Village and deaf to it. During a Roam the living never route to a Ghost: a route that opened to a killed Player would announce a death the Village is not yet allowed to know. Muting a living route at a kill tells only Players who are within Vision of the Body anyway. In Meetings, after every death has been revealed, the living are heard by everyone still in the Game and Ghosts only by Ghosts, which matches who may speak in chat.

**When browsers cannot reach each other, the game server relays.** Over a shared link many Players sit behind carrier-grade or other strict NATs, where direct WebRTC fails even with STUN, and there is no TURN server to fall back on. A TURN service would need an account and credentials. Instead, a client that has no direct connection to a peer encodes its microphone with WebCodecs into 20 ms Opus packets and sends them over the game socket (`voice_frame`). The server relays each packet only to the named targets who may hear the sender at that moment, so a relayed route is enforced by the server itself. The receiver decodes into a small jitter buffer (60 ms to start, never more than 160 ms) and plays it through the same volume and panning as direct audio. A direct connection wins as soon as it connects. Relayed voice is narrower (32 kbit/s), sends nothing during silence and adds the round trip through the host, but it reaches anyone who can reach the Game. Because a relayed frame is useless once late, it is the one outbound message the server may drop: it is discarded rather than queued behind a backlog, and it never takes a place a Game event needs, so a slow link loses a syllable rather than its Game connection. This narrows ADR 0004's never-evict rule to Game events.

**Voice bypasses the frame boundary.** ADR 0003 applies network events only at Phaser frame boundaries, and a hidden tab draws no frames. Voice messages change no world state and drive no Phaser object, so the transport hands them straight to the voice pipeline. A closed route is then followed at once even in a background tab, and negotiation adds no frame of delay. Only the cosmetic loudness and panning are applied per frame from the field.

**Voice belongs to one connection.** Players opt in with `set_voice`; Reconnect, Disconnect and Leave all leave the connection outside voice, and the client re-enters on its next Room Snapshot if its Player had joined. A peer that disappears from the routes and comes back therefore always gets fresh connections, without epochs or reconnection bookkeeping.

**Crisp and immediate.** Opus is negotiated for full-band mono speech at 64 kbit/s with in-band forward error correction and no discontinuous transmission, which would clip the first syllable after a pause. The microphone uses the browser's echo cancellation, noise suppression and gain control. Connections stay open while routes flip, so walking into earshot is a track swap rather than a new negotiation. The voice sender is given high network priority, and playback goes through an interactive-latency audio context.

**Microphones need a secure page.** Browsers grant one only to `https` pages or `localhost`. `run.py` therefore serves the page over https with a self-signed certificate (`PU_TOWN_HTTPS=1`), and the WebSocket follows as `wss` through the same proxy. A shared Cloudflare link is already https. Without a secure page a Player can still listen. Across networks, public STUN servers let most direct connections through, and the rest are relayed by the game server.

Removing voice means removing `set_voice`, `voice_signal`, `voice_frame`, `voice_routes` and `Game.hears` together; nothing else depends on them.
