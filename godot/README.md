# Godot snow prototype

This standalone preview extends the ground milestone from issues #91–93 with
changing snow depth, overlapping bootprints and wind. Two 1.7 m parka Avatars share one snow field; Tab switches which
one you control, and the other walks a loop across the map. Each walks, runs and stands with clips exported by
[`tools/characters/parka`](../tools/characters/parka/README.md); the clips follow the
figure's step phase, its planted boots settle into their Footprints with the clip's
legs, its swinging boots plough through loose snow, and its Footprints use the same stride. Their gloves carry stand-in Player Colours. It runs without Java, LiveKit, a Room or Game state. Sharing
trails between these local figures does not establish multiplayer synchronization.
Existing clients retain their current integration.
[ADR 0025](../docs/adr/0025-godot-single-snow-map-prototype.md) records the engine
and single-map direction;
[ADR 0026](../docs/adr/0026-dynamic-snow-and-persistent-footprints.md) records the
changing surface and persistent world tracks;
[ADR 0027](../docs/adr/0027-reference-snow-town-with-wind-shaped-depth.md) records
saved Snow Depth, wind and Facing.
[ADR 0028](../docs/adr/0028-frostwatch-outpost-flat-snowfield-and-camp.md) records
Frostwatch Outpost, the agreed design that replaces ADR 0027's 100 × 56 m reference
town: a flat 500 × 550 m snowfield with a fenced Camp at its centre, Roads, a traced
pine forest and an oblique camera, after the owner's
[references](../docs/art/frostwatch-reference/README.md). It lands in slices. This
guide describes the delivered interim preview: one flat **160 × 160 m** level of
open snow with the Camp at its centre, placeholder boxes for its lodge, sheds,
cabins, workshop, towers and crate stacks round the Shallow Town Square; four
Shallow Roads from the square's corners to the map's edge, and placeholder tree
clusters standing in Deep Snow beside them; a north-up perspective camera that
follows the controlled figure while the mouse sets its Facing; a saved Snow Depth
that starts Shallow in the Town Square and on the Roads, Deep in the tree
clusters and in between elsewhere; a north-west wind that builds Snowdrifts in
the lee of buildings and trees, scours their windward faces and fills Footprints; a detail region shaped
for the oblique view that keeps bootprints crisp within 20 m of the figure;
a walking
figure whose loop lays trails you did not watch being made; and a blizzard in
overcast polar twilight, tuned live in the editor's Inspector (see
[Lighting and the blizzard](#lighting-and-the-blizzard)). The
[flat interim map record](../docs/verification/2026-10-17-godot-snow-flat-interim-map.md)
holds its import checks, engine-event replay, graphical route in source and the
Mac release package, each kept separate, with their limits; the
[Camp record](../docs/verification/2026-10-18-godot-snow-camp.md) holds the same
evidence for the Camp's buildings, with captures beside the owner's painted Camp,
and the [Roads record](../docs/verification/2026-10-20-godot-snow-roads.md) for the
Roads and Deep tree clusters.

Use the standard **Godot 4.7.2 stable** editor, with GDScript support. Download it
from the [pinned release archive](https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.7.2-stable/).
The project uses the Compatibility (`gl_compatibility`) renderer and opens a
2560 × 1440 window, 16:9 like the owner's reference views; on a Retina Mac that is
1280 × 720 points drawn at full resolution. A screen without room for it gets the
largest 16:9 window that fills nine tenths of it. It has no third-party assets, addons or .NET dependency.
The scene and all geometry are generated by checked-in source; the Avatar's
`characters/parka/parka.glb` is the exported output of a checked-in Blender script,
as are the snow pine's `scenery/pine/pine.glb` and its distance version `pine_far.glb`
([`tools/scenery/pine`](../tools/scenery/pine/README.md)), which the scene does not place yet.

## Run

From the repository root, use the launcher with Godot on your `PATH`:

```sh
./godot/run.sh
```

Or select an executable, command name or macOS application explicitly:

```sh
GODOT_BIN="$HOME/Downloads/Godot.app" ./godot/run.sh
GODOT_BIN=/path/to/godot ./godot/run.sh --check
```

The launcher requires a `4.7.2.stable` version, imports the project headlessly
before every launch, validates every GDScript file and stops on reported errors.
`--check` performs those checks without opening the scene. It does not exercise movement or rendering.
Godot's generated `.godot/` cache remains local.

To use the editor on macOS or Windows, import [`project.godot`](project.godot),
then press F5 to run the configured main scene,
[`snow_walk.tscn`](snow_walk.tscn). Alternatively, run the pinned executable
directly from the repository root:

```sh
/path/to/godot --headless --editor --path godot --import
/path/to/godot --path godot
```

On Windows substitute the downloaded Godot `.exe` path; quote paths containing
spaces. The shell launcher also works in an environment that provides Bash and a
runnable Godot binary. No packaged application or export templates are needed for
this source-project slice.

## Inspect

Walk with WASD at 1.06 m/s, the pace of the owner's walk recording. Directions follow the screen, whose north is always
up: W walks north, S south, D east and A west. Diagonal input is normalized. Hold Shift to request running at up to 4.5 m/s.
Loose snow at or below 10 cm allows full running, while the permitted speed
smoothly falls to walking at 40 cm. Repeated traffic compresses the snow and can
make a faster Packed Path. Alternating boots press into the visible snow; the
figures remain temporary scale and contact references, not final Avatar artwork.

Tab switches between the orange and blue-grey figures. Both contribute anonymous
bootprints to the same world field. Tracks overlap, remain when the detail region
moves away, and fade as wind fills them. A fresh launch resets the snow and both
figures. Both start in the Town Square's Shallow Snow either side of the map's
centre, at its `start_west` and `start_east` landmarks, (−1.5, 0) m and (1.5, 0) m.

### The walking figure

The figure you are not controlling walks a loop without stopping:

centre → east across the Town Square → north → west along its own lane across the
square, south of the lodge → north past the lodge's west end into open snow → back
the same way

It always asks to run, so it runs at full speed in the Town Square's Shallow Snow
and is held back in the deeper open snow beyond it, and it speeds up on Packed
Paths like you do. It faces the way it travels and moves with the same speed,
collision and sliding rules as the controlled figure. Its loop is about 129 m out
and back, about half a minute at its pace.

When you press Tab, the walking figure stops where it is and you take it over. The
figure you released walks in a straight line from where it stands to the nearest
point of the loop it can reach without contact, then carries on around the loop.

Its trail is ordinary Footprints in the shared picture: it persists, fills with
the wind like any other and overlaps yours. The loop's corners are the
`start_east` and `loop_…` landmarks below, listed in `LOOP` in
[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd). It keeps clear of the scene route's trail
probe, Packed Path corridor and wind probes, and of every Camp building and tree.

#### Practise tracking

1. Press `−` to set wind 0, so trails stay as they are while you look for them.
2. Walk away from the walking figure, for example west into the open snow, and
   let it cross the Town Square without watching. The HUD shows where it is and
   how far round the loop it has gone.
3. Head back into the square and look for a line of bootprints you did not make.
   Bootprints point the way it was going; prints in Shallow Snow are spaced
   further apart because it was running.
4. Follow the trail. Press F5 on it to fix a probe, then walk along and over it
   and watch the probe's compaction rise as your prints overlap its own.
5. Restore wind with `0`, or raise it with `=`, and watch the trail fill.

### Deep and Shallow Snow

Snow Depth starts by place, so you can predict where you can run. Loose snow at
or below 10 cm allows full running; from 40 cm you can only walk until repeated
traffic packs a Packed Path.

- **Shallow Snow**, 9 cm: the **Town Square**, the Camp's open yard, 40 × 30 m at
  the centre of the map between the lodge and the cabins, and the four **Roads**,
  4 m wide. Run freely.
- **Open snow**, 25 cm: everywhere else. Running is partly slowed; your own fresh
  prints pack part of the snow you run through, so you reach about 3.8 m/s, and
  repeated traffic packs a faster Packed Path.
- **Deep Snow**, 60 cm: inside the tree clusters. Even with your own fresh prints
  counted you can only walk, until repeated traffic packs a Packed Path.

Each zone fades into its surroundings over 1.5 m, and Shallow wins where a Road
passes a cluster's corner. No place starts below 3 cm. A fresh launch restores this
starting snow; wind then reshapes it.

### Wind, Snowdrifts and Footprints

The prevailing wind blows from the **north-west**. `−` and `=` set its strength
from 0 to 16 and `0` restores the default of 1; `[` and `]` turn the direction it
blows from by 45° anticlockwise or clockwise. The HUD shows the strength and the
compass point it blows from. There is no snowfall: wind only moves the snow that is
already there.

Wind reshapes Snow Depth by relaxation toward a steady shape, so drifts build and
settle instead of growing forever or pulsing:

- **Snowdrifts** build in the lee of obstacles, the side facing away from
  the wind. A drift rises toward the obstacle's height, at most about 1.2 m, right
  behind it and tapers away within three obstacle heights (at most 6 m). Only
  obstacles whose windward side faces open ground build one: an obstacle standing
  in another's shelter receives no blown snow.
- **Scour** thins exposed windward sides, the faces the wind strikes, toward a floor
  of 3 cm: fully within half an obstacle height of the face, fading out by one
  obstacle height, counted as 2–4 m. Snow never scours to bare ground.
- Each place closes about 63% of the gap to its steady shape in 60 wind-seconds:
  a minute at the default wind, or under four seconds at 16. At wind 1 a drift is
  most of the way built after about three minutes.

The Camp's buildings and the placeholder trees are the map's obstacles. The
north-west wind builds a Snowdrift along the lodge's south face, into the Town
Square, and scours the lodge's west face; the small shed stands in the workshop's
shelter, so it gathers no blown snow and builds no drift of its own, and the snow
in its lee keeps its depth while Footprints there last longer. The south-west
cluster's corner tree drifts snow over the south-west Road, so a Road is easier
going but not guaranteed. Open snow away from the buildings and trees keeps its
starting shape. The Perimeter will add more shelter. Turning the
wind recomputes which side of every obstacle is sheltered: old drifts
wear away and new ones build in the new lee. Snowdrifts stay walkable at walking
speed, and running always follows the current loose depth, so a scoured face can
become runnable and a drift walk-only.

A Footprint fills as wind drops snow on it. In the open it fills in **about 120
wind-seconds**: two minutes at the default wind, 7.5 seconds at 16. It fills faster,
up to four times faster, where a drift is building, so drifting snow buries tracks.
In shelter, within two obstacle heights downwind of an obstacle (at most 6 m),
it lasts **up to four times longer**, fading out of shelter by five obstacle heights
(at most 12 m). Wind 0 freezes both drifts and Footprints.

Wind updates the Snow Depth picture four times a second, 27 rows of the
picture per physics frame, and never rewrites the Footprint picture. A turned
wind's new drifts, scour and shelter are shaped on a worker thread, which takes
0.8–1 s on this map; until then the snow keeps following the old shape, and
the HUD marks the wind as turning. Each
place keeps a fill clock that runs at its own rate; a print remembers the fill
clock when it was pressed and fills by how far that clock has run since.

### Camera and Facing

The camera matches the owner's camera reference frames
([record](../docs/verification/2026-10-17-godot-snow-oblique-camera.md)): it looks
down at 40° through a wide 40° vertical field of view, so the figure's body and
the sides of boxes show. By default the 1.7 m figure stands about a twelfth of the
screen tall, 22.6 m from the camera. North is always up and the camera never
turns. Its focus sits a little north of the controlled figure, 3.4% of the
distance, so the figure's feet stand just below the centre of the screen at every
play distance. The focus leans toward the cursor's point on the ground, by a
quarter of the cursor's distance from the figure, so you see further where you
look. How far it may lean grows with the play distance: 3 m at the closest, 5.5 m
by default and 8 m at the widest, in step with the distance, so zoomed out you can
look further ahead along a Road; zooming in shortens a longer lean at once
([record](../docs/verification/2026-10-17-godot-snow-lean-and-sharp-region.md)). The mouse wheel zooms between the figure an eighth of
the screen tall, the references' framing (15.4 m), and a sixteenth (29.7 m);
`follow_camera.gd` derives these distances from the shares. One step out beyond
the widest opens a whole-map overview for inspection, which frames the map
rectangle around its centre without lean; one step in returns to the widest play
distance. **M** opens the overview from any play distance, and M again returns to
the distance you left. A wheel step out does nothing while the overview is open,
and one step in returns to the widest play distance however it was opened.
Walking, the lean and the detail region behave the same whichever way it was opened.

The camera frames the untouched snow where the figure stands, before Footprints
packed it, so Snowdrifts move it exactly as the snow does. Each footfall sinks the figure into the snow it packs; the camera takes only
a fifth of that sink, eased over about 0.2 s (`SINK_SHARE` and `SINK_SETTLE` in
`follow_camera.gd`), so walking reads as a gentle settle at every step rather than
a jolt. The cursor's point on the ground is found at the untouched snow's height
too, so a footfall never moves Facing or the lean.

The cursor's point on the ground sets the bearing the figure wants to look
along. A cursor within 25 cm of the figure, or outside the window, gives no new
bearing. The upper body, from the lower spine to the head, turns toward Facing;
the hips, legs and boots keep stepping in the direction of travel, so bootprints
point where the figure went and speed is unchanged whichever way it faces.

- **Standing still**, Facing points at the cursor exactly. The legs pivot once the
  upper body has turned 60° from them, without stepping, so turning in place
  leaves no prints.
- **Walking**, the upper body turns toward the cursor but no more than **110°**
  from the direction of travel, like the bounded upper-body turn of on-foot
  shooters. With the cursor further round, the figure looks over the shoulder it
  last used and never swings through its back. It changes shoulder only when the
  cursor comes back within 110° on the other side, and then turns through the
  front. Facing turns no faster than **540° per second**, so a change of shoulder
  or a change of travel never snaps it round.
- **Stopping** with Facing still away from the cursor, it turns to the cursor at
  the same rate, with the legs pivoting after it, and then tracks it exactly.

The 110° twist is spread over the spine as before, so the head alone turns about
16° and about 72° counting the neck and upper chest. After Tab, the newly
controlled figure takes its Facing from the cursor; the other faces where it
walks.

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| WASD | Walk relative to the screen: W north, S south, D east, A west; diagonals are normalized |
| Shift | Request running, up to 4.5 m/s where the snow allows it |
| Mouse | Face the cursor's point on the ground; the camera leans toward it, up to 3 m close in and 8 m zoomed out |
| Mouse wheel | Zoom between the figure an eighth and a sixteenth of the screen tall, or one step further out to the whole-map overview; one step in from the overview returns to the widest play distance |
| M | Open the whole-map overview, or return from it to the play distance you left |
| Tab | Switch the controlled figure; the released figure walks back to the loop |
| `−` / `=` | Reduce/increase wind from 0 to 16 in steps of one; keypad minus/plus also work |
| `0` | Restore the default wind strength of 1 |
| `[` / `]` | Turn the wind 45° anticlockwise/clockwise; it starts from the north-west |
| F1 | Toggle the observation HUD |
| F4 | Increment the checkpoint recorded in the observation CSV |
| F5 | Add a fixed world probe at the controlled figure's latest bootprint, or at its position before its first step; up to eight |
| F6 | Record 60 real seconds of rendered-frame intervals |
| F8 | Start/stop inspection observations in `user://route-….csv` |
| F9 | Save the rendered viewport to `user://capture-….png` |

Strength 0 freezes weather and preserves tracks and drifts; strength 16 advances
the same weather sixteen times faster. Movement pauses when the window loses focus,
but the scene and wind keep advancing.

The HUD and CSV expose figure identity, position, the ground height beneath the
figure (`ground_y_m`), Facing, the latest travel and bootprint directions as
compass bearings (`facing_deg`, `travel_deg`, `print_deg`), the upper body's
turn from the legs (`twist_deg`, Facing minus the legs' direction, clockwise
positive), the cursor's ground
point (`cursor_x_m`, `cursor_z_m`), speed, run request, the visible Snow Depth above
that ground (`snow_depth_m`), the saved loose Snow Depth under the figure before
packing (`saved_depth_m`), the loose depth that sets running speed after nearby
packing (`loose_depth_m`), compaction, sinking, contact gap, obstacle/boundary contact, steps,
the camera's focus (`focus_x_m`, `focus_y_m`, `focus_z_m`), the untouched snow's height
where the figure stands (`untouched_y_m`), the detail region's centre and size
(`region_x_m`, `region_z_m`, `region_width_m`, `region_depth_m`) and the world line
where its far part meets its near part (`region_seam_z_m`), the Footprint
picture bytes sent to the GPU in the latest rendered frame (`upload_bytes`), in
total (`upload_total_bytes`) and the footsteps those uploads carried
(`uploaded_steps`), with the size of one tile (`footprint_tile_bytes`), the
Footprint tiles allocated now (`footprint_tiles`), their bytes (`footprint_bytes`)
and the totals created and freed (`footprint_tiles_created`,
`footprint_tiles_freed`), the layers of the picture's GPU copy
(`footprint_gpu_layers`, `footprint_gpu_bytes`) and the bytes sent in all
rebuilding it at a new size (`footprint_gpu_rebuild_bytes`), wind strength, the bearing it blows from (`wind_from_deg`) and
the bearing whose shape the snow follows now (`wind_shaped_from_deg`, which lags a
turn until it is shaped), the
weather clock, the wind's work in the latest physics frame (`wind_update_ms`),
and the walking figure: its identity, position, step count and
progress round the loop as a fraction of its length (`other_loop_progress`, the
point where it will join while `other_rejoining` is true), together with its feet
and ground heights, speed, run request, loose depth, contact, obstruction, Facing
and travel bearings and distance walked (`other_…` columns). Each F5 probe stays at its world
location when you walk away or switch figures. For every probe the CSV reports
`probe_count` and, in columns `probe1_…` to `probe8_…`, its position (`x_m`,
`z_m`), loose Snow Depth (`depth_m`), visible snow above the ground after packing
(`visible_m`), compaction, whether it lies in the detail region (`in_region`) and
whether an allocated Footprint tile holds any texel its compaction is read from
(`in_tile`); unused slots stay empty. The HUD lists the same readings. The CSV
also records input, the camera's projection, field of view, distance, overview
state, lean (`lean_x_m`, `lean_z_m`, `lean_m`), pitch and yaw, framing (where the
feet stand on screen, `figure_screen_x` and `figure_screen_y`, and the 1.7 m
figure's share of the screen's height, `figure_screen_height`) and elapsed time.

Godot writes observations, frame traces and captures into the project's local
application-data directory, outside this checkout. Use the editor's **Project →
Open User Data Folder** to find them. These records support inspection; a CSV or
capture alone is not packaged-platform or hardware performance acceptance.

For a manual check, reduce wind to zero, walk a trail across the Town Square and press
F5. Leave the detail region, then return and check that the trail remains at the
same place. Switch figures and follow or cross it to inspect overlapping prints
and Packed Paths, then find and follow the walking figure's trail as described
under [Practise tracking](#practise-tracking). Compare Shift movement in the
square's Shallow Snow, on a Road, in the open snow and in a tree cluster's Deep
Snow, then increase wind and watch the fixed probe and visible trail fade, and the
south-west Road drift beside the cluster's corner tree. Run along a Road to the map's edge and check that it
stops you without a visible wall, lost in the whiteout. Move the cursor round the
standing figure to turn it in place without prints, then walk while facing
elsewhere and check that the bootprints follow the travel. Walk with the cursor
behind the figure and check that it looks over one shoulder rather than twisting
backwards, and that it changes shoulder through the front. Press M to open and
close the overview. Check close and overview framing, the camera's lean toward the
cursor, snow contact, and the transition between detailed and distant snow while
moving.

The scene route sends keyboard, wheel and mouse-movement events through Godot's input API and
checks the public observation CSV without addressing private scene nodes, mesh
ordering or procedural samples. Its destinations are the named landmarks in
[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd), and its bounds come from the same map rectangle:

```sh
/path/to/godot --path godot -- --snow-scene-check
```

Press **F7** in the scene to begin and keep the window focused. Add `--auto-start`
after `--snow-scene-check` to skip the F7 gate; focus is still required. The route
pauses on focus loss and fails after 60 seconds without focus. Starting at the
centre, it moves the cursor round the standing figure and checks that
Facing points at the cursor's ground point with no new bootprint, leans the
camera toward a corner of the view exactly as far as the default play distance
allows and back, and walks north and
south while facing east, with prints and speed following the travel. Running west
across the Town Square, it runs at full speed in the Shallow Snow and is held back
in the open snow beyond it, on the lane between the Camp's western buildings. West
of the Camp it walks south into the south-west tree cluster, where running in the
fresh Deep Snow is held to walking speed; it runs, walks twice more and runs again
along one lane there, each pass followed by the walk back over it, showing a
measurable Packed Path running advantage over the fresh lane. While the cursor leans the camera toward a corner
and back, the detail region follows the focus and returns with it. Every
observation checks that the region keeps its size with its edges and its seam on

## Lighting and the blizzard

The map sits in a blizzard under overcast polar twilight, after the
[mood references](../docs/art/snow-town-reference/README.md): a whiteout that
swallows the distance, falling snow, snow blowing along the ground, gusts that
thicken both and pull the whiteout closer, a rare diffuse lightning flash, and
eight warm lamps on the Camp's doors and windows facing the Town Square against
the cold light. A dim, cool,
low **SkyKey** light casts faint soft shadows, which keep Footprints readable;
their own shading follows its direction.

The blizzard is only how the town looks. Gusts never change the wind that
shapes Snow Depth, builds Snowdrifts and fills Footprints, so wind 0 still keeps
trails intact during a storm, and fog does not limit what a Player may see.
Falling and blowing snow travel the way the wind blows and turn with `[` and `]`.
Every fog distance scales with the camera's, so play distances and the M
overview show the same haze and the overview stays legible. At the 22.6 m play
distance the fog begins 11 m from the camera and is full at 105 m, so it takes
about an eighth of the figure, a third of the top edge 42 m away and a sixteenth
of the bottom edge 17 m away, as the long lens's fog did before. SkyKey draws a
single shadow map out to 54 m, scaled the same way, a little beyond the view's
far edge; splitting shadows over a longer range cost about 4 ms a frame for nothing.
The snow receives those shadows but casts none: with SkyKey 40° up its smooth
surface cannot shade itself, and drawing it into the map ran its vertex heights a
second time. The scene routes
hold lightning off so their captures stay comparable. The lamps' places are
`WARM_LIGHTS` in [`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd), each 15 cm outside a
building's face at door or window height, 1.8–2.4 m: the lodge's door and two
windows, the generator shed's and workshop's doors, the small shed's window and the
two cabins' doors. There are eight, the Compatibility renderer's lights per object,
so each one reaches the snow; the route checks that each hangs on a Camp building.

### Tune it in the Godot editor

All of it lives in [`blizzard.tscn`](blizzard.tscn), so the editor's Inspector is
its control panel:

1. Open Godot 4.7.2, choose **Import**, select [`project.godot`](project.godot)
   and open the project.
2. In the **FileSystem** dock, double-click `blizzard.tscn`.
3. Press **F5** (Run Project) and arrange the editor beside the game window.
   **Debug → Synchronize Scene Changes** must be ticked (the default).
4. In the **Scene** dock, select a node and drag values in the **Inspector**;
   the running game changes at once:
   - **Blizzard**: grouped Whiteout, Shadows, Snowfall, Blowing snow, Gusts,
     Lightning and Warm lights settings, and the random seed.
   - **SkyKey**: the cool key light's angle (Rotation), Color, Energy and shadow
     Opacity and Blur.
   - **WorldEnvironment → Environment**: Background colour, Ambient Light,
     Tonemap and exposure, Glow, Fog colour and Adjustments. The Blizzard node
     sets the fog's density, depth range and light energy and the background
     energy every frame, so edit those through the Blizzard's Whiteout group,
     and SkyKey's shadow distance, which its Shadows group sets.
   - **Snowfall** and **BlowingSnow**: particle count, lifetime, flake texture
     and material. Changing the amount restarts their particles.
5. Press **Ctrl+S** (⌘S on macOS) to keep the values; the next launch uses them.

Editing nodes in the **Remote** tab of the Scene dock also changes the running
game, but is not saved. Exported packages have no editor, so tune in the editor
and export afterwards. The eight lamps together cost about 1.4 ms per frame in
the [measurement](../docs/verification/2026-10-16-godot-snow-blizzard.md); the
falling snow, blowing snow and fog each cost less than 0.5 ms.

## Reproduce the surface

[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd) defines the map once, as a rectangle with
separate east–west and north–south half extents, together with its named
landmarks, the Camp's buildings and the warm lamps. The snow surface, the shader's
map uniform, obstacle and figure containment, wind shelter, the camera overview
and the scene route all derive from it; do not repeat map dimensions or building
positions in other code. The half extents are 80 m east–west and 80 m
north–south: a 160 × 160 m field spanning −80 to +80 m in X and in Z.

The ground is one flat level, `GROUND_LEVEL` in [`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd),
0 m. [`snow_surface.gd`](snow_surface.gd) and the shader read that same level, so
shader heights and CPU foot support agree everywhere. The snow surface is the
ground plus loose Snow Depth, reduced by Footprint compaction; packing retains at
least 2.5 cm over the ground. Snow Depth is saved state, not a formula: a fixed
world picture of 401 × 401 single-float nodes 0.4 m apart, counted from the map's
west and north edges and read bilinearly, measured vertically above the ground. A
fresh launch saves it from the authored zones in [`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd)
and never loads saved snow. Every rendered vertex and every foot-support query
reads it, with the Footprint picture, each frame.

Bootprints are shaped in height inside a **detail region** that follows the
camera's focus, including the lean, and is shaped for the oblique view: 44 m wide,
from 14.4 m south of the focus to 21.6 m north of it. Its **near part**, from its
south edge to a **seam** 4 m north of the focus, has a **5 cm grid** (880 × 368
cells); its **far part**, on up the view, a **10 cm grid** (440 × 176 cells). A
live 0.8 m grid keeps the whole map's snow visible beyond it. Without lean, the
region holds in full detail all the snow the view shows within 20 m of the figure
at every play distance: along the bottom of the view, 20 m ahead and out to the
sides, which at the widest reach 19.9 m either side just north of the focus. Up
the view, past the seam, the depressions are drawn on 10 cm cells while the
Footprint picture still shades each sole's outline at its own 31.25 mm texels: a
5 cm grid all the way up cost more frame time than walking at 24 fps allows (the
[record](../docs/verification/2026-10-17-godot-snow-lean-and-sharp-region.md)
measures it). Beyond the region, bootprints are shaded from the Footprint picture
without their relief. The figure stands north of the focus only while the camera
leans toward a cursor nearer the bottom of the view, at most 2.6 m at the widest;
with half a coarse cell of snapping and a boot's 0.65 m reach every footstep lands
in the near part, at least 1.6 m inside the region's blended sides at any lean.
The region's corner snaps to coarse vertices counted from the map's west and north
edges, so both grids are world-aligned and the figure's support does not change
when it moves; it may reach over the edge onto the apron. Each cell of the far
part's last row meets two near cells and is split into three triangles that share
the near vertex between them, so the parts meet at the seam without a crack. The
region blends into the coarse surface over a 1.6 m border on every side, beyond the
map's edge into the apron's snow. Coarse cells under the region collapse in the
vertex shader, so the two surfaces never overlap. In the whole-map overview the
focus is the map centre, so the region stays there while the figure walks. The
snow is drawn in chunks, the coarse surface in squares of up to 32 m and the
region in eight columns cut into strips, so the camera skips those outside the
view.
[`snow.gdshader`](snow.gdshader) reevaluates vertex heights from the ground level
and the current field.
CPU foot support evaluates the same heights and triangle interpolation,
including the region border, rather than querying a stale static collision mesh.

The shading normal is not taken from the triangles. It is the height field's own
slope evaluated smoothly: Snow Depth's through a quadratic B-spline over its
0.4 m nodes, over the flat ground, and each depression's from the Footprint
picture. Bilinear Snow Depth changes slope abruptly at every node line, so
triangle normals shaded Snowdrifts and zone edges as straight bands; the smooth
slope keeps them continuous while every height stays bilinear. Sole outlines are
shaded from the Footprint picture everywhere, inside and outside the region, at
the picture's own 31.25 mm texels: its slope tilts the shading normal and darkens
each sole's rim. A pressed print is shaded as a hollow: its floor is dimmed and
cooled by how deep it is pressed, as overcast sky reaches less of it, and a short
march toward the sun across the Footprint picture shades the part its sunward rim
hides. The sun stands behind the play camera, so lit far walls alone made fresh
prints in loose snow read as raised pads. For legibility from above, snow heaped
deeper than about 0.5 m is slightly whiter. All of this changes shading only,
never height or foot support, so distant trails still read.
Horizontal movement separately sweeps a circle against the rectangles in
[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd), with sliding and map containment.

Footsteps paint one world-anchored RGBA8 Footprint picture at **31.25 mm per
texel**, covering the whole map rectangle: 5120 × 5120 texels for the 160 × 160 m
map. It stores compaction and the fill clock at the texel's centre when it was last
pressed; no growing list of steps is retained. Both figures use this same picture,
and moving the detail region does not move or clear its contents.
[`footprint_tiles.gd`](footprint_tiles.gd) holds it only where steps have landed, in
world-anchored **256 × 128 texel tiles** (8 × 4 m, 131,072 bytes each) in rows
from the map's north-west corner. A tile is created blank when a step first presses
it. Each footstep notes, for the tiles it pressed, every Snow Depth node within
0.4 m of the footfall (the nodes whose fill clocks its texels' heights and its
shading read) and the latest of their fill clocks now. Once wind has run every
one of those clocks 120 fill-seconds past that latest value, neither the print's
heights nor its shading show anything, and once that holds for every print in a
tile the tile is freed. After each wind sweep the tiles are checked, four per
physics frame, against clocks published in an earlier frame, so the record always
shows a print filled before its tile goes. Memory therefore follows where the
figures have walked, not the map's area: the scene route, which walks the Camp, a
Deep Snow lane and a Road to the map's edge, peaks at 76 tiles (9.5 MiB) where the
whole-map picture took **104,857,600 bytes** (100 MiB) whatever the number of
steps, and the wind frees 75 of its 92 tiles.

The GPU copy is a texture array with one layer per tile, read through a picture
with one texel per tile of the map that holds the tile's layer, or none. The shader
reads the Footprint picture in blocks of 2 × 2 texels, which nearly always share a
tile, so it looks up a block's layer once and reads nothing more where no tile is
allocated; in source this drew the play view faster than the whole-map picture did
([measured](../docs/verification/2026-10-21-godot-snow-footprint-tiles.md)). A texture
array cannot change size in place, so the copy starts with 8 layers, doubles when a
new tile finds no spare layer and halves once a quarter or fewer of its layers are
in use; each time, every allocated tile is packed into the lowest layers and every
layer is sent once. It never grows past a layer for each of the map's 800 tiles. In
the route it reaches 128 layers (16 MiB) and is back to 32 when the drift stage
ends. A sole spans at most 0.36 m, so a footstep touches at most 2 × 2 tiles; at
the end of each rendered frame only the tiles that frame's footsteps touched are
resent, at most **524,288 bytes per footstep** (four tiles), never the whole
picture. Those figures exclude each tile's list of awaited fill clocks, the
tile-to-layer picture, driver, upload-staging and other scene memory. Wind ages the
encoded values without rewriting the picture each frame;
new footfalls update it. [`snow_wind.gd`](snow_wind.gd) holds the wind: the
working Snow Depth and fill-clock pictures (401 × 401 nodes each), the target
shape for the current direction, marched cell by cell from the obstacles upwind
and downwind of each node and shaped on a worker thread when the wind turns (nodes
inside an obstacle take their open neighbours' mean, so a drift meets the wall
without a trough), and
the sweep that relaxes 27 rows per physics frame. At the end of each sweep the
surface publishes both pictures to its CPU copies and the shader's textures in
the same physics frame, so visible snow and foot support keep agreeing. A fresh process recreates the field rather than loading
saved snow.

The material retains muted snow and overcast lighting. The source image,
complete colour histogram and palette remain authoring references outside the
Godot project; no reference image enters the runtime. The
[initial ground record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-snow-walk.md) and
[static desktop record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-snow-desktop.md)
describe the preceding static milestone. Their results do not establish dynamic
snow behavior, current appearance or performance.

The [dynamic-snow verification record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-dynamic-snow.md)
describes the 64 × 64 m scene that preceded the reference town. The
[town verification record](../docs/verification/2026-10-16-godot-snow-town.md)
describes that 100 × 56 m reference town: the headless and focused graphical scene routes in
the source scene and the Mac release package, ordinary macOS keyboard and mouse
input, inspected captures with the defects they show, a 60-second release frame
trace with the wind's cost, and both exports. The
[shading record](../docs/verification/2026-10-16-godot-snow-shading.md) corrects
those defects and compares before and after captures; its smooth shading adds
about 2 ms per frame in an unfocused source-scene timing and 1.7 ms in the
remeasured release trace.
The [camera steadiness record](../docs/verification/2026-10-16-godot-snow-camera.md)
measures the camera's per-step vertical travel before and after it took only a
softened share of each footfall's sink. The
[flat interim map record](../docs/verification/2026-10-17-godot-snow-flat-interim-map.md)
describes the current 160 × 160 m map.

## Measure a traversal

Launch the **release package**, keep it focused at its 2560 × 1440 window, warm it by walking,
then press F6 and immediately walk for 60 seconds. Cross the Town Square's
Shallow Snow and the open snow beyond it and leave a trail, without standing
against the map's edge. Record the wind setting and any figure switching. The recorder
stops automatically using real elapsed time. Focus loss and stationary intervals
remain in the trace, and are reported rather than filtered. F8 may run alongside
it for detailed contact/camera observations. Avoid F9 capture during the measured
minute. Summarize the saved trace with:

```sh
python3 godot/summarize_frames.py "/path/to/user-data/frames-….csv"
```

Each rendered frame also records the wind's work in the physics frames since the
previous one (`wind_ms`, `wind_updates`) and the wind setting (`wind_strength`,
`wind_from_deg`). The summary's `wind` section reports its cost per physics
update and per rendered frame, its share of frame time and every setting seen;
traces recorded before these columns report `"recorded": false`. Wind 0 costs
almost nothing, so measure at a nonzero strength.

Average FPS is complete rendered-frame intervals divided by their monotonic
wall-clock span. Frame-time p95/p99 use nearest rank over every interval. These
are engine frame-loop timings, including recording overhead, rather than GPU
timestamps or display presentation latency. The report includes duration,
focus/movement coverage, resolution and observation age so invalid runs can be
identified. Keep the raw trace with engine/mode, hardware, OS and concurrent
workload notes; HUD FPS and fixed-rate route CSV are not benchmark substitutes.

The [lean and sharp-region record](../docs/verification/2026-10-17-godot-snow-lean-and-sharp-region.md)
holds the latest release traces, of the flat interim map at the 2560 × 1440
window on an Apple M1 under macOS 26.2, posted as ordinary macOS events by a
driver that stops when the package loses the front (its input script and runner
are in the record's folder). In the one pair taken while the load eased, the
current build averaged 27.9 FPS against the previous revision's 30.4 FPS, about
2.9 ms a frame more; under memory pressure from other applications both fell to
21–24 FPS. The
[shading record](../docs/verification/2026-10-16-godot-snow-shading.md) holds the
reference town's traces at 1280 × 800, where its smooth shading cost about 1.7 ms
per frame; compare packages within one session rather than across records. These are one machine
with a desktop workload, not a platform baseline. The
[#92–93 record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-snow-desktop.md) measured
the older static scene and is not a current result. Rebuild and remeasure after
source changes. Final town assets, Game/networking integration and coordinated
retirement of the existing maps remain later milestones. The Windows export has
not been run; Windows runtime and hardware performance require running on
Windows hardware.
