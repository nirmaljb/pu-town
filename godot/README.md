# Godot snow prototype

This standalone preview extends the ground milestone from issues #91–93 with
changing snow depth, overlapping bootprints, wind and a town made of solid box
placeholders. Two 1.7 m parka Avatars share one snow field; Tab switches which
one you control. Each walks, runs and stands with clips exported by
[`tools/characters/parka`](../tools/characters/parka/README.md); the clips follow the
figure's step phase, its boots are bent onto the snow under them, and its
Footprints use the same stride. Their gloves carry stand-in Player Colours. It runs without Java, LiveKit, a Room or Game state. Sharing
trails between these local figures does not establish multiplayer synchronization.
Existing clients retain their current integration.
[ADR 0025](../docs/adr/0025-godot-single-snow-map-prototype.md) records the engine
and single-map direction;
[ADR 0026](../docs/adr/0026-dynamic-snow-and-persistent-footprints.md) records the
changing surface and persistent world tracks.
[ADR 0027](../docs/adr/0027-reference-snow-town-with-wind-shaped-depth.md) records
the agreed rebuild as the 100 × 56 m town of the
[whole-map reference](../docs/art/snow-town-reference/README.md). This guide
describes its first slice: the 100 × 56 m town laid out from that reference as
solid placeholder boxes, still on flat ground with the existing snow, camera and
idle second figure. Ground levels and the stair, saved Snow Depth with wind
drifts, the perspective camera and the walking second figure follow later.

Use the standard **Godot 4.7.2 stable** editor, with GDScript support. Download it
from the [pinned release archive](https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.7.2-stable/).
The project uses the Compatibility (`gl_compatibility`) renderer and opens at
1280 × 800. It has no third-party assets, addons or .NET dependency.
The scene and all geometry are generated by checked-in source; the Avatar's
`characters/parka/parka.glb` is the exported output of a checked-in Blender script.

## Run

From the repository root, use the launcher with Godot on your `PATH`:

```sh
./godot/run.sh
```

Or select an executable, command name or macOS application explicitly:

```sh
GODOT_BIN="$HOME/Downloads/Godot.app" ./godot/run.sh
GODOT_BIN=/path/to/godot ./godot/run.sh --check
```

The launcher requires a `4.7.2.stable` version, imports the project headlessly
before every launch, validates every GDScript file and stops on reported errors.
`--check` performs those checks without opening the scene. It does not exercise movement or rendering.
Godot's generated `.godot/` cache remains local.

To use the editor on macOS or Windows, import [`project.godot`](project.godot),
then press F5 to run the configured main scene,
[`snow_walk.tscn`](snow_walk.tscn). Alternatively, run the pinned executable
directly from the repository root:

```sh
/path/to/godot --headless --editor --path godot --import
/path/to/godot --path godot
```

On Windows substitute the downloaded Godot `.exe` path; quote paths containing
spaces. The shell launcher also works in an environment that provides Bash and a
runnable Godot binary. No packaged application or export templates are needed for
this source-project slice.

## Inspect

Walk with WASD at 2.25 m/s. Directions follow the camera's horizontal axes;
diagonal input is normalized. Hold Shift to request running at up to 4.5 m/s.
Loose snow at or below 10 cm allows full running, while the permitted speed
smoothly falls to walking at 40 cm. Repeated traffic compresses the snow and can
make a faster Packed Path. Alternating boots press into the visible snow; the
figures remain temporary scale and contact references, not final Avatar artwork.

Tab switches between the orange and blue-grey figures, leaving the other idle.
Both contribute anonymous bootprints to the same world field. Tracks overlap,
remain when the detailed patch moves away, and fade as wind fills them. A fresh
launch resets the snow and both figures. Both start in the outpost yard, at its
`outpost_yard_west` and `outpost_yard_east` landmarks, (22, 4) m and (28, 4) m,
where shallow and deep snow mix.

### The town

The town follows the composition of the whole-map reference, with north up (−Z):

- **Walled compound** (west). Its ranges, including the long hall on the north
  side, the bell tower, outer walls and gatehouse enclose a courtyard of about
  24 × 13.5 m with a well pavilion and two pines. Reach the courtyard through
  either 3 m gate: the east gate faces the meeting hall, and the gatehouse's
  south gate faces the forest.
- **Meeting hall** in the central gap, at its reference place and shape. The gaps
  on its west, north and east sides stay passable.
- **Outpost** (east). The lodge, two sheds, workshop, radio tower, generator,
  crates and barrels, and fences surround an open yard.
- **Rim and forest**. Rock masses close the north, east and south-east rim, and
  pines fill the southern forest and the ground north of the compound. An open
  lane runs south from the gatehouse.

Every placeholder is a solid, non-enterable box anchored to the flat substrate,
with a size and colour that distinguish its kind. Walk around them; their roofs
are not walking surfaces, and generators have no interaction. The reference guides
the composition, not measured dimensions or a final asset layout. The route's
named landmarks are, in metres:

| Landmark | X, Z | Place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `outpost_yard_west` | 22, 4 | Outpost yard; figure 1 starts here |
| `outpost_yard_east` | 28, 4 | Outpost yard; figure 2 starts here |
| `lodge_front` | 32, −2 | Yard south of the lodge |
| `meeting_hall_gap_south` | −6, 7.5 | South end of the gap between compound and meeting hall |
| `meeting_hall_gap` | −6, 0 | That gap, outside the east gate |
| `east_gate` | −9.25, 0 | The compound's east gate |
| `courtyard_east` | −12, 0 | Courtyard, inside the east gate |
| `courtyard` | −22, 0 | Middle of the courtyard |
| `courtyard_west` | −30, 3 | Deep snow in the west of the courtyard |

The orthographic camera starts at 45° yaw, −55° pitch and a 6 m view size, close
enough to read the Avatar with a little of the town around it.
Use the mouse wheel to zoom from 3.5 m to a 118 m whole-map view. At the widest
view it frames the ground's centre; as the view closes it blends toward following
the figure.

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `−` / `=` | Reduce/increase wind from 0 to 16 in steps of one; keypad minus/plus also work |
| `0` | Restore the default wind strength of 1 |
| F1 | Toggle the observation HUD |
| F4 | Increment the checkpoint recorded in the observation CSV |
| F5 | Fix a world probe at the controlled figure's latest bootprint, or at its position before its first step |
| F6 | Record 60 real seconds of rendered-frame intervals |
| F8 | Start/stop inspection observations in `user://route-….csv` |
| F9 | Save the rendered viewport to `user://capture-….png` |

At wind strength 1 a print's compaction fades over 120 seconds without another
step there. Strength 0 freezes weather and preserves tracks; strength 16 advances
that same weather sixteen times faster. Wind also changes broad snow relief and
accumulation beside selected obstacles. Movement pauses when the window loses
focus, but the scene and wind keep advancing.

The HUD and CSV expose figure identity, position, speed, run request, surface and
loose depth, compaction, sinking, contact gap, obstacle/boundary contact, steps,
the moving patch, wind, image allocation and the other figure's position. The F5
probe stays at its world location when you walk away or switch figures; its depth,
compaction and whether it lies in the detailed patch remain observable. The CSV
also records input, camera orientation, framing, focus and elapsed time.

Godot writes observations, frame traces and captures into the project's local
application-data directory, outside this checkout. Use the editor's **Project →
Open User Data Folder** to find them. These records support inspection; a CSV or
capture alone is not packaged-platform or hardware performance acceptance.

For a manual check, reduce wind to zero, walk a trail across the outpost yard and press
F5. Leave the detailed patch, then return and check that the trail remains at the
same place. Switch figures and follow or cross it to inspect overlapping prints
and Packed Paths. Compare Shift movement in shallow and deep snow, approach the
lodge diagonally to inspect sliding along its sides, then increase wind and watch
the fixed probe and visible trail fade. Check close and overview framing, snow
contact, and the transition between detailed and distant snow while moving.

The scene route sends keyboard and wheel events through Godot's input API and
checks the public observation CSV without addressing private scene nodes, mesh
ordering or procedural samples. Its destinations are the named landmarks in
[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd), and its bounds come from the same map rectangle:

```sh
/path/to/godot --path godot -- --snow-scene-check
```

Press **F7** in the scene to begin and keep the window focused. Add `--auto-start`
after `--snow-scene-check` to skip the F7 gate; focus is still required. The route
pauses on focus loss and fails after 60 seconds without focus. Starting from the
outpost yard, it walks and runs in shallow and deep snow and is stopped by the
lodge. It lays a probed trail in the meeting-hall gap, walks through the east gate
into the courtyard, is stopped by the courtyard wall and crosses the courtyard.
It then shows a Packed Path's running advantage in the courtyard's deep snow. The
second figure walks from the outpost yard to overlap the trail. The first figure
returns through the gate to find the trail retained, watches wind erase it, and
walks south through the forest until the map edge stops it. Finally it zooms to
the whole-map overview and back, capturing graphical views along the way. Every
navigation leg must reach its landmark. The process returns nonzero on failure. `--walk-scene-check` remains an alias; the
existing `--script res://tests/walk_scene.gd` launcher opens the same route.

An explicit headless replay is also available:

```sh
/path/to/godot --headless --path godot -- --snow-scene-check --headless-scene-check
```

Headless replay runs automatically and does not render captures. It can check
observed scene behavior but supplies no visual, native input, window-focus or
hardware-rendering evidence. Graphical engine-event replay likewise complements
ordinary window input and visual inspection; it does not establish OS keyboard
behavior or a performance baseline.

## Reproduce the surface

[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd) defines the map once, as a rectangle with
separate east–west and north–south half extents, together with its named
landmarks. The snow surface, the shader's map uniform, obstacle and figure
containment, the camera overview and the scene route all derive from it; do not
repeat map dimensions elsewhere. The half extents are 50 m east–west and 28 m
north–south: a 100 × 56 m field spanning −50 to +50 m in X and −28 to +28 m in Z.

[`snow_surface.gd`](snow_surface.gd) defines a flat substrate at Y=0 beneath that
snow field. Snow begins
with broad shallow/deep variation and responds to wind and compaction. The
uncompressed depth is bounded to 5–85 cm; packing retains at least 2.5 cm over the
substrate. The yard remains open without being flattened.

A 12.8 × 12.8 m patch uses a 0.1 m grid around the controlled figure; a 0.8 m grid
keeps the full town visible outside it. The patch moves in coarse-grid increments
and stays within the map, blending into the coarse surface over a 1.6 m border.
[`snow.gdshader`](snow.gdshader) reevaluates vertex heights from the current field.
CPU foot support evaluates the same height formula and triangle interpolation,
including the patch border, rather than querying a stale static collision mesh.
Horizontal movement separately sweeps a circle against the rectangles in
[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd), with sliding and map containment.

Footsteps paint one world-anchored RGBA8 image at **31.25 mm per texel**, sized
to cover the whole map rectangle: **3200 × 1792** for the 100 × 56 m town. It stores compaction and the last wind-clock
timestamp at each texel; no growing list of steps is retained. Both figures use
this same image, and moving the patch does not move or clear its contents.
The CPU image pixel payload is **22,937,600 bytes** (about 21.9 MiB), whatever the
number of steps. The GPU texture is a separate nominal allocation of the same size; those figures exclude driver, upload-staging and other scene
memory. Wind ages the encoded values without rewriting the image each frame;
new footfalls update it. A fresh process recreates the field rather than loading
saved snow.

The material retains muted snow and overcast lighting. The source image,
complete colour histogram and palette remain authoring references outside the
Godot project; no reference image enters the runtime. The
[initial ground record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-snow-walk.md) and
[static desktop record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-snow-desktop.md)
describe the preceding static milestone. Their results do not establish dynamic
snow behavior, current appearance or performance.

The [dynamic-snow verification record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-dynamic-snow.md)
records the current scene route, native input and visual checks, exports and
remaining packaged-runtime and hardware evidence limits.

## Desktop exports

Install the standard **4.7.2 stable export templates** through Godot's
**Editor → Manage Export Templates**, or extract `macos.zip`,
`windows_release_x86_64.exe` and `windows_debug_x86_64.exe` from the official
`Godot_v4.7.2-stable_export_templates.tpz` into Godot's `export_templates/4.7.2.stable/`
directory. On macOS that directory is under
`~/Library/Application Support/Godot/`. Then run:

```sh
GODOT_BIN="$HOME/Downloads/Godot.app" ./godot/export.sh all
open godot/build/macos/release/PU-Town-Snow-Walk.app
```

Use `macos` or `windows` instead of `all` for one target; append `--debug` for
a debug package. The release Mac app is universal Intel/Apple Silicon, ad-hoc
signed for local preview and not notarized for public distribution. The Windows
x86-64 executable embeds its project data. Both use Compatibility rendering and
1280 × 800; the Mac preset disables Retina scaling for this measurement target.
On Windows, run the pinned editor's `--headless --path godot --export-release
"Windows Preview" <output.exe>` after import, or use the Bash script where Bash
and Python 3 are installed. Launch the resulting `.exe` normally.

`godot/build/` is ignored. Each completed output contains `export.log` and
`build-info.json` with the engine, renderer, build mode, source hashes and package
hashes. Failed exports preserve the previous completed output. Generated packages,
templates and archives stay local under the upload-size policy.

The packaged application exposes the same snow inspection route:

```sh
open godot/build/macos/release/PU-Town-Snow-Walk.app --args -- --snow-scene-check
```

Start from a fresh launch, press F7 and keep it focused for the route. It exits
with a status after checking the exported observations. This is engine-event
replay in the package; ordinary keyboard walking is separate evidence. Rebuild
packages after source changes and check their manifests before treating them as
evidence for the current implementation.

## Measure a traversal

Launch the **release package**, keep it focused at 1280 × 800, warm it by walking,
then press F6 and immediately walk for 60 seconds. Cross shallow and deep snow,
leave a trail, and turn around the boxes without standing against an obstacle or
boundary. Record the wind setting and any figure switching. The recorder
stops automatically using real elapsed time. Focus loss and stationary intervals
remain in the trace, and are reported rather than filtered. F8 may run alongside
it for detailed contact/camera observations. Avoid F9 capture during the measured
minute. Summarize the saved trace with:

```sh
python3 godot/summarize_frames.py "/path/to/user-data/frames-….csv"
```

Average FPS is complete rendered-frame intervals divided by their monotonic
wall-clock span. Frame-time p95/p99 use nearest rank over every interval. These
are engine frame-loop timings, including recording overhead, rather than GPU
timestamps or display presentation latency. The report includes duration,
focus/movement coverage, resolution and observation age so invalid runs can be
identified. Keep the raw trace with engine/mode, hardware, OS and concurrent
workload notes; HUD FPS and fixed-rate route CSV are not benchmark substitutes.

The [#92–93 verification record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-snow-desktop.md)
separates visual inspection, packaged runtime, performance and export evidence
for the older static scene. New dynamic-snow source requires its own runtime,
visual and performance checks; the earlier measurements are not current results.
Final town assets, Game/networking integration and coordinated retirement of the
existing maps remain later milestones. Windows hardware performance requires
running on Windows hardware.
