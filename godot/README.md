# Godot snow prototype

This standalone preview extends the ground milestone from issues #91–93 with
changing snow depth, overlapping bootprints, wind and a town made of solid box
placeholders. Two 1.7 m parka Avatars share one snow field; Tab switches which
one you control. Each walks, runs and stands with clips exported by
[`tools/characters/parka`](../tools/characters/parka/README.md); the clips follow the
figure's step phase, its boots are bent onto the snow under them, and its
Footprints use the same stride. Their gloves carry stand-in Player Colours. It runs without Java, LiveKit, a Room or Game state. Sharing
trails between these local figures does not establish multiplayer synchronization.
Existing clients retain their current integration.
[ADR 0025](../docs/adr/0025-godot-single-snow-map-prototype.md) records the engine
and single-map direction;
[ADR 0026](../docs/adr/0026-dynamic-snow-and-persistent-footprints.md) records the
changing surface and persistent world tracks.
[ADR 0027](../docs/adr/0027-reference-snow-town-with-wind-shaped-depth.md) records
the agreed rebuild as the 100 × 56 m town of the
[whole-map reference](../docs/art/snow-town-reference/README.md). This guide
describes its first five slices: the 100 × 56 m town laid out from that reference
as solid placeholder boxes, on a town shelf about 5 m above the southern forest
and joined to it by the snow-buried stair; a north-up perspective camera that
follows the controlled figure while the mouse sets its Facing; a saved Snow
Depth that starts Deep or Shallow by place; and a north-west wind that builds
Snowdrifts, scours windward sides and fills Footprints faster or slower by place.
The second figure stays idle; the walking second figure follows later.

Use the standard **Godot 4.7.2 stable** editor, with GDScript support. Download it
from the [pinned release archive](https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.7.2-stable/).
The project uses the Compatibility (`gl_compatibility`) renderer and opens at
1280 × 800. It has no third-party assets, addons or .NET dependency.
The scene and all geometry are generated by checked-in source; the Avatar's
`characters/parka/parka.glb` is the exported output of a checked-in Blender script.

## Run

From the repository root, use the launcher with Godot on your `PATH`:

```sh
./godot/run.sh
```

Or select an executable, command name or macOS application explicitly:

```sh
GODOT_BIN="$HOME/Downloads/Godot.app" ./godot/run.sh
GODOT_BIN=/path/to/godot ./godot/run.sh --check
```

The launcher requires a `4.7.2.stable` version, imports the project headlessly
before every launch, validates every GDScript file and stops on reported errors.
`--check` performs those checks without opening the scene. It does not exercise movement or rendering.
Godot's generated `.godot/` cache remains local.

To use the editor on macOS or Windows, import [`project.godot`](project.godot),
then press F5 to run the configured main scene,
[`snow_walk.tscn`](snow_walk.tscn). Alternatively, run the pinned executable
directly from the repository root:

```sh
/path/to/godot --headless --editor --path godot --import
/path/to/godot --path godot
```

On Windows substitute the downloaded Godot `.exe` path; quote paths containing
spaces. The shell launcher also works in an environment that provides Bash and a
runnable Godot binary. No packaged application or export templates are needed for
this source-project slice.

## Inspect

Walk with WASD at 2.25 m/s. Directions follow the screen, whose north is always
up: W walks north, S south, D east and A west. Diagonal input is normalized. Hold Shift to request running at up to 4.5 m/s.
Loose snow at or below 10 cm allows full running, while the permitted speed
smoothly falls to walking at 40 cm. Repeated traffic compresses the snow and can
make a faster Packed Path. Alternating boots press into the visible snow; the
figures remain temporary scale and contact references, not final Avatar artwork.

Tab switches between the orange and blue-grey figures, leaving the other idle.
Both contribute anonymous bootprints to the same world field. Tracks overlap,
remain when the detailed patch moves away, and fade as wind fills them. A fresh
launch resets the snow and both figures. Both start in the outpost yard, at its
`outpost_yard_west` and `outpost_yard_east` landmarks, (22, 4) m and (28, 4) m:
the first in the yard's Shallow Snow, the second in its eastern Deep drift.

### The town

The town follows the composition of the whole-map reference, with north up (−Z):

- **Walled compound** (west). Its ranges, including the long hall on the north
  side, the bell tower, outer walls and gatehouse enclose a courtyard of about
  24 × 13.5 m with a well pavilion and two pines. Reach the courtyard through
  either 3 m gate: the east gate faces the meeting hall, and the gatehouse's
  south gate faces the forest.
- **Meeting hall** in the central gap, at its reference place and shape. The gaps
  on its west, north and east sides stay passable.
- **Outpost** (east). The lodge, two sheds, workshop, radio tower, generator,
  crates and barrels, and fences surround an open yard.
- **Rim and forest**. Rock masses close the north, east and south-east rim, and
  pines fill the southern forest and the ground north of the compound. An open
  lane runs south from the foot of the stair.

### Two levels and the stair

The compound, meeting hall and outpost stand on the **town shelf**, at ground
height 0 m. The **southern forest**, west of x −5.5 m and south of z 10 m, lies
5 m lower, at −5 m. The two levels meet only in two ways:

- **The stair** continues the compound's 3 m south gate. It is one snow-buried
  slope, 10 m long from z 9 m to z 19 m, with no individual steps and nothing
  walkable beneath it. Low walls run along both sides, each 1 m segment standing
  on the slope beneath it, so you cannot step off. Slope never changes speed:
  walking stays 2.25 m/s on the way down and back up.
- **Cliff rock** covers every other step between the levels: a band along the
  shelf's southern edge, two masses flanking the stair, and a mass along the
  forest's east side. Each rises 2 m above the shelf and stops you from either
  level. The step in the ground lies at least one coarse snow cell inside each
  mass, so no stretched snow face shows in normal views.

Every placeholder is a solid, non-enterable box standing on the ground of its
level, with a size and colour that distinguish its kind. A box rises from the
lowest ground beneath it to its height above the highest; only cliff rock spans
both levels. Walk around them; their roofs
are not walking surfaces, and generators have no interaction. The reference guides
the composition, not measured dimensions or a final asset layout. The route's
named landmarks are, in metres:

### Deep and Shallow Snow

Snow Depth starts by place, so you can predict where you can run. Loose snow at
or below 10 cm allows full running; from 40 cm you can only walk until repeated
traffic packs a Packed Path.

- **Shallow Snow**, 9 cm: the compound's courtyard, and the stair from its south
  gate down to the forest floor. Run freely.
- **Mixed**: the outpost yard is Shallow, with Deep drifts in its east, around
  `outpost_yard_east`, and along the lodge's south face. Your first steps cross
  both.
- **Deep Snow**, 60 cm: the forest floor, within 2 m of any cliff rock on either
  level, and the meeting-hall gap around the meeting hall, between the compound
  and the outpost. Walk only.
- **Elsewhere** on the shelf, such as north of the compound and around the rim,
  the snow starts at 25 cm, where running is partly slowed.

Each zone fades into its surroundings over 1.5 m, and no place starts below 3 cm.
Where zones overlap, the courtyard and stair stay Shallow beside the cliffs that
flank them. A fresh launch restores this starting snow; wind then reshapes it.

### Wind, Snowdrifts and Footprints

The prevailing wind blows from the **north-west**. `−` and `=` set its strength
from 0 to 16 and `0` restores the default of 1; `[` and `]` turn the direction it
blows from by 45° anticlockwise or clockwise. The HUD shows the strength and the
compass point it blows from. There is no snowfall: wind only moves the snow that is
already there.

Wind reshapes Snow Depth by relaxation toward a steady shape, so drifts build and
settle instead of growing forever or pulsing:

- **Snowdrifts** build in the lee of boxes and cliffs, the side facing away from
  the wind. A drift rises toward the obstacle's height, at most about 1.2 m, right
  behind it and tapers away within three obstacle heights (at most 6 m). Only
  obstacles whose windward side faces open ground build one: an obstacle standing
  in another's shelter receives no blown snow.
- **Scour** thins exposed windward sides, the faces the wind strikes, toward a floor
  of 3 cm: fully within half an obstacle height of the face, fading out by one
  obstacle height, counted as 2–4 m. Snow never scours to bare ground.
- Each place closes about 63% of the gap to its steady shape in 60 wind-seconds:
  a minute at the default wind, or under four seconds at 16. At wind 1 a drift is
  most of the way built after about three minutes.

With the prevailing wind the lodge's south face and the south-east sides of exposed
buildings and rim rocks drift deep, while exposed north-west faces such as the east
shed's west face scour to 3 cm. Turning the wind recomputes which side
of every obstacle is sheltered: old drifts wear away and new ones build in the new
lee. Snowdrifts stay walkable at walking speed, and running always follows the
current loose depth, so a scoured face can become runnable and a drift walk-only.

A Footprint fills as wind drops snow on it. In the open it fills in **about 120
wind-seconds**: two minutes at the default wind, 7.5 seconds at 16. It fills faster,
up to four times faster, where a drift is building, so drifting snow buries tracks.
In shelter, within two obstacle heights downwind of a box or cliff (at most 6 m),
it lasts **up to four times longer**, fading out of shelter by five obstacle heights
(at most 12 m). **Tracks last longest in sheltered places**: the compound's
courtyard, the walled stair and the lee of large buildings once their drifts have
settled. Wind 0 freezes both drifts and Footprints.

Wind updates the Snow Depth picture four times a second, ten rows of the
picture per physics frame, and never rewrites the Footprint picture. Each
place keeps a fill clock that runs at its own rate; a print remembers the fill
clock when it was pressed and fills by how far that clock has run since.

### Camera and Facing

The camera is perspective, with a 40° vertical field of view and a −55° pitch,
16 m from its focus. North is always up and the camera never turns. Its focus
sits a little north of the controlled figure, so the figure stands slightly below
the centre of the screen at every play distance. The focus leans toward the
cursor's point on the ground, by a quarter of the cursor's distance from the
figure and never more than 3 m, so you see further where you look. The mouse
wheel zooms from 10 to 30 m. One step out beyond 30 m opens a whole-map overview
for inspection, which frames the map rectangle around its centre without lean;
one step in returns to 30 m.

The figure faces the cursor's point on the ground, including while standing
still. A cursor within 25 cm of the figure, or outside the window, leaves Facing
as it was. The upper body, from the lower spine to the head, turns toward Facing;
the hips, legs and boots keep stepping in the direction of travel, so bootprints
point where the figure went and speed is unchanged whichever way it faces.
Standing still, the legs pivot once the upper body has turned 60° from them,
without stepping, so turning in place leaves no prints. After Tab, the newly
controlled figure takes its Facing from the cursor; the other keeps its own.

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| WASD | Walk relative to the screen: W north, S south, D east, A west; diagonals are normalized |
| Shift | Request running, up to 4.5 m/s where the snow allows it |
| Mouse | Face the cursor's point on the ground; the camera leans up to 3 m toward it |
| Mouse wheel | Zoom from 10 to 30 m, or one step further out to the whole-map overview |
| Tab | Switch the controlled figure |
| `−` / `=` | Reduce/increase wind from 0 to 16 in steps of one; keypad minus/plus also work |
| `0` | Restore the default wind strength of 1 |
| `[` / `]` | Turn the wind 45° anticlockwise/clockwise; it starts from the north-west |
| F1 | Toggle the observation HUD |
| F4 | Increment the checkpoint recorded in the observation CSV |
| F5 | Add a fixed world probe at the controlled figure's latest bootprint, or at its position before its first step; up to six |
| F6 | Record 60 real seconds of rendered-frame intervals |
| F8 | Start/stop inspection observations in `user://route-….csv` |
| F9 | Save the rendered viewport to `user://capture-….png` |

Strength 0 freezes weather and preserves tracks and drifts; strength 16 advances
the same weather sixteen times faster. Movement pauses when the window loses focus,
but the scene and wind keep advancing.

The HUD and CSV expose figure identity, position, the ground height beneath the
figure (`ground_y_m`), Facing, the latest travel and bootprint directions as
compass bearings (`facing_deg`, `travel_deg`, `print_deg`), the cursor's ground
point (`cursor_x_m`, `cursor_z_m`), speed, run request, the visible Snow Depth above
that ground (`snow_depth_m`), the saved loose Snow Depth under the figure before
packing (`saved_depth_m`), the loose depth that sets running speed after nearby
packing (`loose_depth_m`), compaction, sinking, contact gap, obstacle/boundary contact, steps,
the moving patch, wind strength, the bearing it blows from (`wind_from_deg`), the
weather clock, the wind's work in the latest physics frame (`wind_update_ms`),
image allocation and the other figure's position. Each F5 probe stays at its world
location when you walk away or switch figures. For every probe the CSV reports
`probe_count` and, in columns `probe1_…` to `probe6_…`, its position (`x_m`,
`z_m`), loose Snow Depth (`depth_m`), visible snow above the ground after packing
(`visible_m`), compaction and whether it lies in the detailed patch (`in_patch`);
unused slots stay empty. The HUD lists the same readings. The CSV
also records input, the camera's projection, field of view, distance, overview
state, lean (`lean_x_m`, `lean_z_m`, `lean_m`), pitch and yaw, framing, focus and
elapsed time.

Godot writes observations, frame traces and captures into the project's local
application-data directory, outside this checkout. Use the editor's **Project →
Open User Data Folder** to find them. These records support inspection; a CSV or
capture alone is not packaged-platform or hardware performance acceptance.

For a manual check, reduce wind to zero, walk a trail across the outpost yard and press
F5. Leave the detailed patch, then return and check that the trail remains at the
same place. Switch figures and follow or cross it to inspect overlapping prints
and Packed Paths. Compare Shift movement in shallow and deep snow, approach the
lodge diagonally to inspect sliding along its sides, then increase wind and watch
the fixed probe and visible trail fade. Lay probes in the courtyard, in the open
outpost yard and against the lodge's south face, raise the wind to 16 and compare
how fast each print fills while the lodge's drift grows; then turn the wind with
`]` and watch the drift wear away. Walk out of the compound's south gate,
down the stair to the forest and back up, and check that boots stay on the snow,
the HUD's ground height changes by 5 m and the cliff rock stops you on both
levels. Move the cursor round the standing figure to turn it in place without
prints, then walk while facing elsewhere and check that the bootprints follow the
travel. Check close and overview framing, the camera's lean toward the cursor,
snow contact, and the transition between detailed and distant snow while moving.

The scene route sends keyboard, wheel and mouse-movement events through Godot's input API and
checks the public observation CSV without addressing private scene nodes, mesh
ordering or procedural samples. Its destinations are the named landmarks in
[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd), and its bounds come from the same map rectangle:

```sh
/path/to/godot --path godot -- --snow-scene-check
```

Press **F7** in the scene to begin and keep the window focused. Add `--auto-start`
after `--snow-scene-check` to skip the F7 gate; focus is still required. The route
pauses on focus loss and fails after 60 seconds without focus. Starting from the
outpost yard, it moves the cursor round the standing figure and checks that
Facing points at the cursor's ground point with no new bootprint, leans the
camera to its 3 m bound toward a corner of the view and back, and walks north and
south while facing east, with prints and speed following the travel. Running east
across the outpost yard, it runs at full speed in the Shallow Snow and only walks
in the Deep drift, and it is stopped by the lodge. In the meeting-hall gap it
runs, walks and runs again along one lane of Deep Snow, showing a measurable
Packed Path running advantage over the fresh lane. It lays a probed trail in the
gap, walks through the east gate into the courtyard, is stopped by the courtyard
wall, crosses the courtyard and runs at full speed in its Shallow Snow. Tab, with the
cursor west of the figure, gives the second figure a westward Facing; it then
walks from the outpost yard to overlap the trail. The first figure
returns through the gate to find the trail retained, and walks south until the
cliff rock at the shelf's edge stops it. It then crosses
the courtyard to the south gate and walks down the stair to the forest,
observing 5 m of ground change, a contact gap within 2 mm and unchanged walking
speed on the slope. On the forest floor, requested running only walks in the Deep
Snow, both toward the map edge and to the foot of the cliff beside the stair;
each stops it, and it climbs the stair back to the gate. Wind stays at 0 until
then. The figure next fixes four more probes: sheltered in the courtyard
(`courtyard_shelter`), exposed in the open outpost yard (`yard_open`), on the east
shed's windward west face (`shed_windward`) and in the lee of the lodge's south
face (`lodge_lee`). At wind 16 from the north-west the exposed print fills at the
120 wind-second rate, the sheltered one four times more slowly and the lee one
faster still; the lodge's drift grows from 60 cm toward 1.2 m without shrinking
and settles, and the shed's face scours to the 3 cm floor and holds. The figure
wades through the drift with Shift held at walking speed. Four turns of `]`
reverse the wind: the lodge's drift wears away and one builds on the shed's face.
Wind 0 then freezes every probe's depth and compaction. Finally it zooms to
the 30 m end of the play range, on to the whole-map overview, which must frame the
town, and back in to 10 m, capturing graphical views along the way. Throughout, the
camera stays a 40° perspective view at −55° pitch with north up, its lean stays
within 3 m, its play distance within 10–30 m, and without lean it holds the
figure slightly below the centre of the screen. Every
navigation leg must reach its landmark. The process returns nonzero on failure. `--walk-scene-check` remains an alias; the
existing `--script res://tests/walk_scene.gd` launcher opens the same route.

An explicit headless replay is also available:

```sh

```

Headless replay runs automatically and does not render captures. It can check
observed scene behavior but supplies no visual, native input, window-focus or

## Reproduce the surface

[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd) defines the map once, as a rectangle with
separate east–west and north–south half extents, together with its named
landmarks. The snow surface, the shader's map uniform, obstacle and figure
containment, the camera overview and the scene route all derive from it; do not
repeat map dimensions elsewhere. The half extents are 50 m east–west and 28 m
north–south: a 100 × 56 m field spanning −50 to +50 m in X and −28 to +28 m in Z.

Ground height is static. At launch, [`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd) builds a
fixed world ground-height picture from the authored levels and the stair's one
slope: 501 × 281 single-float nodes 0.2 m apart, read bilinearly. Its boxes stand
on that picture, and [`snow_surface.gd`](snow_surface.gd) and the shader read the
same nodes with the same interpolation. The snow surface is ground height plus
loose Snow Depth, reduced by Footprint compaction; packing retains at least 2.5 cm
over the ground. Snow Depth is saved state, not a formula: a fixed world picture
of 251 × 141 single-float nodes 0.4 m apart, counted and read bilinearly like the
ground picture, measured vertically above the ground. A fresh launch saves it from
the authored zones in [`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd) and never loads saved
snow. Every rendered vertex and every foot-support query reads it, with the
Footprint picture, each frame.

A 12.8 × 12.8 m patch uses a 0.1 m grid around the controlled figure; a 0.8 m grid
keeps the full town visible outside it. The patch moves in coarse-grid increments
and stays within the map, blending into the coarse surface over a 1.6 m border.
[`snow.gdshader`](snow.gdshader) reevaluates vertex heights from the ground picture
and the current field.
CPU foot support evaluates the same heights and triangle interpolation,
including the patch border, rather than querying a stale static collision mesh.
Horizontal movement separately sweeps a circle against the rectangles in
[`town_layout.gd`](town_layout.gd), with sliding and map containment.

Footsteps paint one world-anchored RGBA8 image at **31.25 mm per texel**, sized
to cover the whole map rectangle: **3200 × 1792** for the 100 × 56 m town. It stores compaction and the fill clock
at the texel's centre when it was last pressed; no growing list of steps is retained. Both figures use
this same image, and moving the patch does not move or clear its contents.
The CPU image pixel payload is **22,937,600 bytes** (about 21.9 MiB), whatever the
number of steps. The GPU texture is a separate nominal allocation of the same size; those figures exclude driver, upload-staging and other scene
memory. Wind ages the encoded values without rewriting the image each frame;
new footfalls update it. [`snow_wind.gd`](snow_wind.gd) holds the wind: the
working Snow Depth and fill-clock pictures (251 × 141 nodes each), the target
shape for the current direction, marched cell by cell from the obstacles upwind
and downwind of each node and shaped on a worker thread when the wind turns, and
the sweep that relaxes ten rows per physics frame. At the end of each sweep the
surface publishes both pictures to its CPU copies and the shader's textures in
the same physics frame, so visible snow and foot support keep agreeing. A fresh process recreates the field rather than loading
saved snow.

The material retains muted snow and overcast lighting. The source image,
complete colour histogram and palette remain authoring references outside the
Godot project; no reference image enters the runtime. The
[initial ground record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-snow-walk.md) and
[static desktop record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-snow-desktop.md)
describe the preceding static milestone. Their results do not establish dynamic
snow behavior, current appearance or performance.

The [dynamic-snow verification record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-dynamic-snow.md)
records the current scene route, native input and visual checks, exports and
remaining packaged-runtime and hardware evidence limits.

## Desktop exports

Install the standard **4.7.2 stable export templates** through Godot's
**Editor → Manage Export Templates**, or extract `macos.zip`,
`windows_release_x86_64.exe` and `windows_debug_x86_64.exe` from the official
`Godot_v4.7.2-stable_export_templates.tpz` into Godot's `export_templates/4.7.2.stable/`
directory. On macOS that directory is under
`~/Library/Application Support/Godot/`. Then run:

```sh
GODOT_BIN="$HOME/Downloads/Godot.app" ./godot/export.sh all
open godot/build/macos/release/PU-Town-Snow-Walk.app
```

Use `macos` or `windows` instead of `all` for one target; append `--debug` for
a debug package. The release Mac app is universal Intel/Apple Silicon, ad-hoc
signed for local preview and not notarized for public distribution. The Windows
x86-64 executable embeds its project data. Both use Compatibility rendering and
1280 × 800; the Mac preset disables Retina scaling for this measurement target.
On Windows, run the pinned editor's `--headless --path godot --export-release
"Windows Preview" <output.exe>` after import, or use the Bash script where Bash
and Python 3 are installed. Launch the resulting `.exe` normally.

`godot/build/` is ignored. Each completed output contains `export.log` and
`build-info.json` with the engine, renderer, build mode, source hashes and package
hashes. Failed exports preserve the previous completed output. Generated packages,
templates and archives stay local under the upload-size policy.

The packaged application exposes the same snow inspection route:

```sh
open godot/build/macos/release/PU-Town-Snow-Walk.app --args -- --snow-scene-check
```

Start from a fresh launch, press F7 and keep it focused for the route. It exits
with a status after checking the exported observations. This is engine-event
replay in the package; ordinary keyboard walking is separate evidence. Rebuild
packages after source changes and check their manifests before treating them as
evidence for the current implementation.

## Measure a traversal

Launch the **release package**, keep it focused at 1280 × 800, warm it by walking,
then press F6 and immediately walk for 60 seconds. Cross shallow and deep snow,
leave a trail, and turn around the boxes without standing against an obstacle or
boundary. Record the wind setting and any figure switching. The recorder
stops automatically using real elapsed time. Focus loss and stationary intervals
remain in the trace, and are reported rather than filtered. F8 may run alongside
it for detailed contact/camera observations. Avoid F9 capture during the measured
minute. Summarize the saved trace with:

```sh
python3 godot/summarize_frames.py "/path/to/user-data/frames-….csv"
```

Average FPS is complete rendered-frame intervals divided by their monotonic
wall-clock span. Frame-time p95/p99 use nearest rank over every interval. These
are engine frame-loop timings, including recording overhead, rather than GPU
timestamps or display presentation latency. The report includes duration,
focus/movement coverage, resolution and observation age so invalid runs can be
identified. Keep the raw trace with engine/mode, hardware, OS and concurrent
workload notes; HUD FPS and fixed-rate route CSV are not benchmark substitutes.

The [#92–93 verification record](../docs/verification/2026-10-15-godot-snow-desktop.md)
separates visual inspection, packaged runtime, performance and export evidence
for the older static scene. New dynamic-snow source requires its own runtime,
visual and performance checks; the earlier measurements are not current results.
Final town assets, Game/networking integration and coordinated retirement of the
existing maps remain later milestones. Windows hardware performance requires
running on Windows hardware.
